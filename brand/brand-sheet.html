0.1
The idea in three steps
STEP 1
A request goes out
One arrow pointing right: the client sends a request. A bar with a 45° head, a 3-unit stroke and round ends, on a 32-unit grid.
STEP 2
A response comes back
A second arrow, 10 units lower, points back: the server answers. ZIO HTTP models HTTP as a function from a request to a response, on the server and in the client.
STEP 3
The arrows become the Z
Top bar, diagonal, bottom bar: the pair traces a Z (red dashes). Set in front of “IO HTTP” it is the first letter of ZIO, and it takes the mint accent.

Brand sheet · 01 · Logo

A round trip that draws a Z

Two arrows on a 32-unit grid: a request goes out along the top, a response comes back along the bottom. Read as one shape they trace the Z of ZIO, so in the wordmark they are the first letter. On its navy tile the mark is the app icon; without the tile it is the Z of the wordmark. It is one colour in every version.
1.1
Symbol
Primary. Mint #3DDC97 on the dark page #06080F, 11.32:1. Dark mode is the default on the site, so this is the version most people see.
Light grounds. Deep mint #0B7A50, 5.37:1 on white. Bright mint is too faint on white (1.77:1), so it never sits on a light ground.
Tile. Navy #0B1020 with the mint mark at full size (icon-tile.svg). For app icons, avatars and any ground you do not control.
1.2
Construction
Grid. 32 units square, the favicon’s pixel grid. All points sit on whole or half units.
Bars. Two bars 20 units long and 10 units apart: the top one at y 11.5 from x 5 to 25, the bottom one at y 21.5 from x 27 back to 7 (red dashes). Each tail runs 2 units past the other arrow’s tip, so the outline is centred on x 16.
Arrowheads. Two legs from each tip, 4.5 units back and 4.5 units out: 45° to the bar (right-hand detail). Top points right, bottom points left.
Stroke. 3 units, round caps and round joins (radius 1.5, dashed circles). No fills: the whole mark is four open paths.
Tile. Navy 32-unit square, corner radius 7 (22 %), mark at full size. The maskable icon drops the corner and holds the mark at 60 % of the canvas.

Wherever the full name appears, the mark is the Z of “ZIO”; it never sits beside a full “ZIO HTTP”, because that would say Z twice. The wordmark uses a cut of the mark cropped to its outline: the arrows sit on the baseline and reach the cap height of Inter Tight Bold (0.728 em), they are 0.822 em wide, and 0.055 em separates them from the I. Their stroke is 0.127 em, the weight of the T’s crossbar, so the Z matches the letters it leads. “IO HTTP” follows in Inter Tight Bold at −0.03 em tracking: ink text and deep mint arrows on light, #E8EAF0 and mint on dark. Horizontal is the default. Stacked puts “ZIO” over “HTTP”, the same size with a line height of 1, for narrow columns, sidebars and conference badges. One colour sets arrows and text in the same ink.

4.5
Rules
Sentence case. Headings and buttons are sentence case: “Get started”, not “Get Started”. Capitals only in mono labels.
Names in mono. Type, method and artifact names in running text are set in JetBrains Mono: Routes, Handler, Server.serve.
Ligatures off. Every mono setting turns ligatures off, so -> stays two characters, the way readers type it.
Inter Tight above, Inter below. Brand material and the hero use Inter Tight; site content below the hero stays in Inter.
Mono is never body copy. Mono is for code, versions, URLs and labels. A paragraph in mono reads as output, not prose.
Write the name as ZIO HTTP. Two words, both in capitals; never “Zio Http” or “ZIO-HTTP”. zio-http is the artifact, zio.http the package.
Brand sheet · 05 · Voice and tone

Name the mechanism, then show it

The reader is a working Scala engineer deciding whether to adopt a library, and will check every claim against the code. ZIO HTTP treats a server and a client as a function from a request to a response, so the voice names the request, the route and the response, and shows them in code before it explains them.
5.1
Voice
The four principles of the existing ZIO HTTP sheet, unchanged. Each card ends with a line that follows it.
01
State the mechanism
Say what the library does and how. Name the type, the method, the guarantee.
Not: blazing-fast, next-generation, effortless.
Middleware.timeout(5.seconds) answers a slow request with 408 Request Timeout.
02
Show code early
A snippet argues better than a paragraph. Lead sections with the smallest example that compiles.
Not: three paragraphs of positioning before the first import.
Method.GET / "greet" -> handler(…) before the paragraph about routing.
03
Quantify or stay quiet
Performance claims carry a number, a version and a benchmark you can link to.
Not: significantly faster than alternatives.
“[n] requests per second on [version], [hardware]. Benchmark: [link].”
04
Name the trade-off
Where the library is opinionated, say so and say why. Engineers trust documentation that admits limits.
Not: silence on the parts that are hard.
Server.serve takes Routes[R, Response]: turn your error type into a response first.
5.2
Tone by context
The voice stays the same. The tone moves along one line as the reader's situation changes: the closer they are to a failing request, the shorter and more exact the words.
ERRORS AND LOGS
Short and exact
Which request, which route, what status, and what the reader can change. No apology, no blame.
RELEASE NOTES
Factual
What changed, who it affects, how to migrate, with the PR number. No adjectives.
DOCUMENTATION
Direct and patient
Code first, one idea per paragraph, every type named in mono. Explain the why once.
TALKS
Clear and warm
One idea per slide, live code over screenshots, and the trade-offs said out loud.
SOCIAL
Friendly and brief
One idea, one snippet or link. A pun on requests is fine; a speed claim without a benchmark is not.
EXACTFRIENDLY
5.3
Say this, not that
Every claim names its mechanism and its limit. The technical lines below were checked against the zio-http source and docs; check new ones the same way before publishing.
Performance
Say “ZIO HTTP runs on Netty. Here are the numbers for this version, and the benchmark that produced them.”
Not “Ultra-fast.” “The most performant library.”
Type safety
Say “An Endpoint describes the path, query and output types. Its implementation is type-checked against that description.”
Not “Bugs become impossible.”
Errors
Say “Server.serve accepts Routes[R, Response]. Map your error type to a Response with handleError before you serve.”
Not “Errors are handled for you.”
Timeouts
Say “routes @@ Middleware.timeout(5.seconds) answers any request that takes longer with status 408.”
Not “Your server never hangs.”
Client and server
Say “The client and the server use the same Request and Response types, so a test can run a request through your routes with runZIO, without opening a socket.”
Not “Seamless full-stack integration.”
5.4
In practice
A docs intro and a release note written to these rules. The release note restates a real fix, PR #4310; the other two are samples, not claims.
DOCS INTRO · ROUTING
A route pairs a pattern, such as Method.GET / "greet", with a handler that turns a Request into a Response. Collect your routes in Routes and pass them to Server.serve. A request that matches no route gets 404 Not Found.
RELEASE NOTE · #4310

Brand sheet · 06 · Visual and imagery style

Request out, response back

ZIO HTTP treats a server and a client as a function from a request to a response. Every picture is built from that round trip: the two arrows of the mark, stretched into lanes, with stations where something happens. Flat, ink and one mint, no photos, no glowing globes.
6.1
Graphic language
Four constructions, all taken from the mark. Lines keep the mark's proportions at any length: the stroke sets the head (1.5 × stroke on each leg, at 45°) and the gap between lanes (10/3 × stroke). Round caps and joins everywhere.
PART 1
The round trip
The two arrows of the mark, stretched. The request goes out on top, to the right; the response comes back below, to the left.
PART 2
Lanes
Each arrow is a lane. The method and path ride above the request lane, the status below the response lane, in mono.

PART 3
The cropped mark
The mark, large and cropped by one edge, one step off the ground colour, behind a heading. Once per layout; the request arrowhead stays whole.
PART 4
Hops
A call made while handling a request is its own pair, stepped down and to the right. Mint marks the hop the picture is about.

Diagrams
The routing path from the README's greeting server: request, Routes, handler, Response, drawn with the two lanes. Stations stand between the lanes, and the lanes stay straight.
Same parts as the mark. Lanes are the mark's two arrows; a reader who knows the logo can read the diagram.
Request on top, going right. Response below, going left. Never swap the lanes or stand them on end.
Labels in mono. Methods, paths, status codes and type names in JetBrains Mono 12 to 13 px; capitals only for column names.
Colour means the subject. Lanes in ink; deep mint (mint on dark) for the one thing the diagram is about, here the matched route.
Layers are dashed. Middleware is a dashed column that both lanes cross, because it sees the request and the response.
Panels, not boxes. Stations are #F5F7FA panels with a 1 px line, radius 14. No shadows, no 3D servers.

Every value is from theme-tokens.css; the two font tokens are a proposal, the file has none yet. Use the role names, not the hex values, so a page switches theme in one place. Names start with --zh- so they never collide with another ZIO library’s tokens on a shared page. The light comment colour, #6B7385, is 4.4:1 on --zh-surface and 4.8:1 on white: darken it to #646C7E (4.9:1), as the Colour board proposes, so comments pass AA and stay a shade apart from secondary text.
